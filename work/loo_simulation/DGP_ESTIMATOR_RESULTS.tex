\begin{abstract}
This report is written for a reader who has read the original LOO project
proposal but has not worked with this simulation.  It therefore keeps the
proposal's model and estimand notation, while defining every simulation-specific
DGP, implementation choice, diagnostic, and comparison rule.

This experiment applies four worker--firm procedures to panels generated by
five different data-generating processes (DGPs).  The procedures are the
heteroskedastic Kline--Saggio--S\o lvsten correction (KSS-HE), the
Bonhomme--Lamadon--Manresa grouped model (BLM), the
Borovi\v{c}kov\'{a}--Shimer procedure (BS20), and the project's current
low-rank plug-in.  Each DGP contains 25,000 workers, 5,000 firms, 60 periods,
and 100 independent Monte Carlo replications.  Because the complete wage
schedule and assignment law are known in a simulation, every estimate can be
compared with an exact finite-population truth.

The expected specification benchmarks hold: KSS-HE is accurate under the
additive AKM DGP, and BLM is accurate under the discrete BLM DGP.  BIC selects
the true interaction rank in all 500 simulated populations.  The project
plug-in is accurate under AKM and GKLP and is accurate in the typical
replication of every nonadditive DGP.  Its unconditional RMSE is nevertheless
poor under Crippa, BLM, and the continuous rank-two DGP because a small set of
ill-conditioned schedule completions creates enormous squared-error tails.
Thus the main unresolved issue for the project benchmark is numerical
regularization of continuous-factor completion, not rank selection in this
design.  The full project leave-one-out correction is still under development
and is not represented as complete here.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, notation, and estimands}

The proposal's wage interface is
\begin{equation}
Y_{ij}=X_{ij}'\delta+\alpha_i+\psi_j+u_i'\Lambda v_j+
\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\Lambda)=r.
\label{eq:proposal-model}
\end{equation}
The simulation sets $X_{ij}'\delta=0$ so that all comparisons concern the
worker effect, firm effect, and nonadditive interface.  It does not change the
model: $m_{ij}=\E[Y_{ij}\mid i,j]$ denotes the systematic part of
Equation~\eqref{eq:proposal-model}.  The panel adds a period index only to
describe which firm is observed: $J_{it}$ is worker $i$'s employer in period
$t$, and $Y_{it}=m_{iJ_{it}}+\varepsilon_{it}$.

The experiment asks one symmetric question: what happens when every procedure
is applied under every DGP?  Using only an additive DGP would mechanically
favor additive methods and would not test comparative advantage or other
nonadditive wage schedules.  The design therefore crosses five DGPs with all
four procedures.  One replication draws a new finite population, samples one
panel, runs every procedure, and compares each reported quantity with the
truth for that same population.

Table~\ref{tab:notation} defines every symbol used below.  A prime denotes
transposition.  All expectations and variances are finite sums, not
large-population approximations.

For computing the proposal estimands from an arbitrary completed schedule, it
is useful to write the product-weighted ANOVA decomposition
\begin{align}
\mu&=\E_{pq}[m_{ij}],
&a_i&=\E_q[m_{ij}\mid i]-\mu,\\
b_j&=\E_p[m_{ij}\mid j]-\mu,
&h_{ij}&=m_{ij}-\mu-a_i-b_j. \label{eq:anova}
\end{align}
The weighted margins of $h_{ij}$ are zero.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\QF=\Var_q(b_j)+\E_{pq}[h_{ij}^2]
=\Var_q(b_j)+\frac{\HF}{2}. \label{eq:qdecomp}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:qdecomp} is derived directly by substituting
Equation~\eqref{eq:anova} into the definition of $\QF$ and using the zero
margins.  The auxiliary letters $(a_i,b_j,h_{ij})$ do not replace the
proposal's structural symbols; for a centered low-rank DGP they correspond to
$(\alpha_i,\psi_j,u_i'\Lambda v_j)$.  The decomposition explains why $\QF$
and $\HF$ are both needed: $\QF$ measures all
firm-side schedule variation, while $\HF$ isolates the part that changes with
the worker.  $\rhoH$ expresses that interaction component as a unit-free
share.  $\Cassign$ answers a different question: how much observed matching
changes schedule variance relative to independent matching with the same
worker and firm marginals.

The exact merged output contains 33,900 scalar estimates and 4,300 procedure
attempts.  Its configuration fingerprint is
\texttt{e075e50934629f7ae20971667f117c418f3628bf576653f079602a07eb76a735}.
Every numerical statement in this report is computed from that output.
Statements taken from papers or software documentation are cited; uncited
equations are definitions, algebraic consequences of those definitions, or
declared simulation choices.

\section{Simulation design}

\subsection{Common population, assignment, and panel}

Every population contains $N=25{,}000$ workers and $J=5{,}000$ firms.  The
firm count was fixed by the production specification.  The lower endpoint of
the proposed 25,000--50,000 worker range was chosen to keep each replication
feasible on the cluster while retaining five workers per firm on average.
Every
panel contains $T=60$ periods, or 1.5 million wage observations before
procedure-specific cleaning.  Worker and firm population marginals are
uniform.  The disturbance is independent
$\varepsilon_{it}\sim N(0,0.5^2)$.  The master seed is 20260812, and every
replication uses separately derived population, panel, and estimator seeds.
The noise standard deviation 0.5 is one half of the standardized main-effect
standard deviation: it creates meaningful observation noise without making
the systematic schedule negligible.

All Gaussian main effects are standardized to finite-population mean zero and
variance one.  Factor columns are centered and orthonormalized under their
population weights.  Consequently, the singular values listed below hold in
each realized finite population rather than only in expectation.

Observed assignment probabilities are
\begin{equation}
P^{\mathrm{obs}}_{ij}=R_iS_j\exp\!\left\{0.5a_ib_j
+0.3\frac{h_{ij}}{\sqrt{\E_{pq}[h_{ij}^2]}}\right\},
\label{eq:assignment}
\end{equation}
where the interaction term is omitted when $h_{ij}=0$.  Positive balancing
constants $R_i$ and $S_j$ restore the uniform marginals.  Thus 0.5 controls
sorting on common worker and firm components, and 0.3 controls sorting on
match-specific gains.  They were chosen to generate visible sorting on both
channels without concentrating assignment on a nearly deterministic set of
matches.  Restoring the same marginals is what lets $\Cassign$ isolate the
joint assignment pattern rather than a change in the worker or firm
population.

Let $\pi_{ij}=P^{\mathrm{obs}}_{ij}/p_i$.  Worker $i$'s period-zero firm is drawn from
$\pi_i$.  At each later period, the worker remains at the current firm with
probability 0.6 and redraws from $\pi_i$ with probability 0.4.  Because a
redraw can return the same firm, the realized probability of an unchanged
firm is slightly above 0.6.  This design gives roughly the same expected
number of redraw opportunities as 120 periods with 0.8 retention, but with a
shorter, more mobile panel.

The 60-period, 0.6-retention design was fixed after a one-replication mobility
gate verified that the rank-two fit retained the full population and passed
the declared numerical diagnostic.  It was not selected after looking at the
100-replication accuracy results.  The 100 replications were split across 50
independent cluster shards and merged only after checking the configuration
fingerprint, replication indices, and absence of duplicates.  One hundred
replications balance tail detection with the cost of fitting all procedures;
they are sufficient to expose the rare failures that a small pilot missed.

The complete $25{,}000\times5{,}000$ schedule and assignment matrix are known
for every population.  Project truths are computed over all 125 million
worker--firm cells before the panel is sampled.  Hence ``truth'' below is not
an estimate from the observed matches.

The underlying draws are fully specified as follows.  AKM and Crippa draw
raw worker and firm effects independently from $N(0,1)$.  The continuous
low-rank DGP draws each raw factor as
$\widetilde u_{i\ell}=0.35\widetilde\alpha_i+
\sqrt{1-0.35^2}\,z_{i\ell}$ and
$\widetilde v_{j\ell}=0.25\widetilde\psi_j+
\sqrt{1-0.25^2}\,z_{j\ell}$, with all innovations standard normal before
normalization.  The GKLP pairs $(Z_i,\eta_i)$ and $(c_j,b_j)$ are jointly
standard Gaussian with correlations 0.35 and 0.25.  In the BLM DGP, worker
and firm labels are assigned as evenly as integer counts allow and then
shuffled; type main effects and raw type factors are independent standard
Gaussian draws before weighted normalization.  Therefore the BLM cell table
is redrawn in every replication rather than fixed by hand.

The five schedules have distinct jobs.  AKM is the additive implementation
check.  Crippa is the simplest smooth rank-one violation of additivity.  The
BLM DGP makes the grouped model exactly specified.  The continuous rank-two
DGP directly instantiates the proposal's low-rank interface.  GKLP supplies an
economically motivated comparative-advantage surface that is low rank but was
not constructed from the project's estimator.  Together they distinguish
correct specification, discrete approximation, rank selection, and numerical
completion.

The Crippa and GKLP rows adapt the cited functional forms to a finite
simulation.  Normalization, sorting coefficients, and latent-variable
distributions are declared design choices rather than claims from those
papers.  Separating this DGP code from estimator code is essential: no
estimator receives the true latent variables or the complete schedule.

Table~\ref{tab:truths} reports the average exact proposal estimands generated
by these choices.  It is included before estimator results so the reader can
separate the scale of a target from the size of an estimation error.  The
interaction normalizations make $\QF$, $\HF$, and $\rhoH$ essentially fixed
within each DGP.  $\Cassign$ varies across replications because it also depends
on the realized sorting kernel.

\section{Procedures and evaluation rules}

\subsection{KSS-HE}

KSS corrects plug-in variance components in linear models with many fixed
effects using leave-out methods; KSS-HE allows unrestricted
heteroskedasticity \cite{kss2020}.  PyTwoWay 0.3.21 supplies the implementation
with approximate trace and leverage calculations
($\texttt{fe\_exact=False}$) \cite{pytwoway}.  Approximation is used because
exact leverage calculations are impractical at 1.5 million observations; the
same setting is held fixed across DGPs.  Its native population target is
the assignment-weighted additive projection
\begin{equation}
(\mu,\alpha,\psi)=\arg\min_{\mu,\alpha,\psi}
\E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[(m_{ij}-\mu-\alpha_i-\psi_j)^2]. \label{eq:kssprojection}
\end{equation}
KSS natively reports
$V_{\psi}^{\mathrm{AKM}}=\Var_q(\psi_j^{\mathrm{AKM}})$ and
$C_{\psi\alpha}^{\mathrm{AKM}}=
\Cov_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{AKM}},
\psi_j^{\mathrm{AKM}})$.  Under nonadditivity these need not equal $\QF$ and
$\Cassign$.  Additivity also imposes $\HF=\rhoH=0$; these are structural zeros,
not estimated interaction moments.  The uncorrected AKM-FE and homoskedastic
KSS-HO outputs remain in the CSV files, but KSS-HE is the displayed KSS column
because it makes the weaker error-variance assumption.  Its leave-one-spell
connected cleaning retained all 1.5 million observations in this design.

\subsection{BLM}

BLM represents the wage distribution using discrete worker types and firm
classes \cite{blm2019}.  The experiment estimates two worker types and three
firm classes using PyTwoWay 0.3.21 \cite{pytwoway}.  These counts make BLM
correctly specified under the grouped DGP and deliberately coarse under the
continuous DGPs.  Firm classes are clustered
from wage-CDF features computed using all 60 periods.  The static likelihood
then uses only periods 0 and 1: worker $i$ is a stayer if $j_{i0}=j_{i1}$ and a
mover otherwise.  Later moves cannot change that label.  This separation
prevents the earlier error of treating only permanent 60-period stayers as
BLM stayers.  The full panel estimates firm wage distributions as precisely as
possible, while the declared pair matches the static BLM mover/stayer
likelihood.

The likelihood uses ten CDF evaluation points, four initializations, the best
two retained starts, at most 250 iterations, and tolerance $10^{-6}$.  These
settings preserve several optimization starts while keeping 500 BLM fits
computationally feasible; they are held fixed across DGPs.  Before
optimization, the code requires all three stayer classes and all nine mover
origin--destination class pairs because PyTwoWay's probability arrays require
those cells.  All 500 production samples passed this support check.  Each BLM
fit uses 50,000 static-period observations; the full panel is used only for
firm clustering.

Latent class labels have no intrinsic ordering.  For simulation scoring, the
estimated $2\times3$ mean table is relabeled by the permutation minimizing
cell-mean squared error relative to a known evaluation table.  Under the BLM
DGP, that table uses the simulated groups.  Under continuous DGPs,
deterministic equal-count groups are ordered by product-marginal mean wages.
These truth-based labels are used only after estimation.  Project functionals
are applied to the aligned cell table using known population cell weights.
Outside the discrete BLM DGP, comparison with the full project truth therefore
includes the deliberate approximation of a continuous schedule by six cells.

\subsection{BS20}

BS20 estimates moments of worker and firm wage types \cite{bs2020}.  The
PyTwoWay weighted estimator is applied after strongly connected, spell-level,
no-return cleaning \cite{pytwoway}.  Its native covariance is
\begin{equation}
\CBS=\Cov_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}(\lambda_i^{\mathrm{BS}},
\mu_j^{\mathrm{BS}}),\qquad
\rhoBS=\operatorname{Corr}_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}(\lambda_i^{\mathrm{BS}},
\mu_j^{\mathrm{BS}}), \label{eq:bsnative}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_i^{\mathrm{BS}}=\E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[m_{ij}\mid i]$ and
$\mu_j^{\mathrm{BS}}=\E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[m_{ij}\mid j]$, matching the
proposal notation.
The code reads PyTwoWay's \texttt{cov(lambda, mu)} directly; it does not
construct it from project quantities.  Because $\CBS\ne\Cassign$ in general,
BS20 is evaluated against its native target and appears in the target-gap
display, not as an estimator of $\Cassign$.  Its cleaning retains about 1.491
million observations on average, depending slightly on the DGP.

\subsection{Project low-rank plug-in}

The current project benchmark fits
\begin{equation}
m_{ij}=\alpha_i+\psi_j+u_i'\Lambda v_j,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\Lambda)=r. \label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
Observed periods are collapsed to worker--firm match means and weighted by
match counts.  A positive-rank alternating least-squares (ALS) fit uses six
exploratory starts.  Three additional starts perturb and refit the best basin
found in that exploration.  Factors are centered and SVD-normalized after
every ALS iteration, so the numerical tail documented below is not caused by
omitted centering.  The tolerance is $10^{-6}$ and the iteration cap is 300.
The split between broad exploration and local confirmation was fixed in the
preflight so that reproducibility can be checked near the best observed basin
without paying for nine full exploratory fits in every rank candidate.

At rank $r$, a worker update estimates one intercept and $r$ factor
coordinates.  Algebra therefore requires at least $r+1$ linearly independent
firm partners.  The code requires $r+2$, one extra partner, as a numerical
safeguard.  All rank candidates use the core required by the largest
candidate: degree three for candidate set $\{0,1\}$ and degree four for
$\{0,1,2\}$.  The production design retains all 25,000 workers, 5,000 firms,
and 1.5 million observations.

BIC selects the rank:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{BIC}(r)=n\log(\operatorname{SSE}_r/n)
+\operatorname{df}(r)\log n,
\qquad
\operatorname{df}(r)=N+J-1+r(N+J-2-r). \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
Candidates are $\{0,1\}$ under AKM and BLM and $\{0,1,2\}$ under Crippa,
continuous low rank, and GKLP.  Equation~\eqref{eq:bic} is an exploratory
simulation choice, not a result from the unfinished LOO theory.  The candidate
sets include every rank present in the DGP ladder and an underfitted
alternative; rank two is structurally impossible with only two BLM worker
types, and ranks above two are outside the experiment's declared scope.  The selected
fit completes unobserved schedule entries and applies the project definitions
to that completion.  This is a plug-in estimator without the unfinished
leave-worker-out bias correction.

For replication $s$, define $e_s=\widehat\theta_s-\theta_s$.  Among $S$
returned values,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Bias}=S^{-1}\sum_se_s,\qquad
\operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{S^{-1}\sum_se_s^2},\qquad
\operatorname{MCSE}(\operatorname{Bias})=
\frac{\operatorname{sd}(e_s)}{\sqrt S}. \label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
Bias records direction; RMSE is the main accuracy measure because it includes
both bias and dispersion and penalizes large errors.  Since the four
estimands have different scales, comparisons are made within each metric.
MCSE quantifies simulation noise in a reported bias; it does not measure the
sampling uncertainty of an estimator in one empirical data set.

The status categories are deliberately separate from accuracy.  KSS and BS20
``pass'' when PyTwoWay returns a value.  BLM also requires complete static
support and no one-step likelihood decline exceeding $10^{-4}$.  The project
best fit must converge.  For positive rank, at least two starts must lie within
$0.0001\max(1,O^*)$ of the best objective $O^*$, and the near-best spreads in
$\QF$, $\HF$, and $\Cassign$ must each be no larger than
$0.001\max(1,|\theta^*|)$.  These are engineering reproducibility thresholds,
not statistical critical values.  The objective rule identifies genuinely
competitive basins, while a one-thousandth functional tolerance makes two
fits equivalent at the precision used in the result tables.  Since $\rhoH$
is derived from $\QF$ and $\HF$, it is not given a redundant spread test.
All-returned RMSE is the unconditional
headline; pass-only RMSE is labeled as a diagnostic sensitivity calculation.

The displays follow a fixed logical order.  Status comes first because an
accuracy number cannot reveal whether a procedure ran reliably.  Own-model
benchmarks then check implementation.  Common-target RMSE answers the main
cross-procedure question.  Native-minus-project gaps separate estimand
disagreement from sampling error.  Rank selection isolates model selection.
Finally, all-returned versus pass-only RMSE identifies whether warnings capture
tail risk.

$-0.00126$ ($0.00217$) for $\Cassign$.

The project plug-in is accurate without conditioning under AKM and GKLP.  Its
pass-only RMSEs are also small under Crippa, BLM, and continuous low rank.
Those conditional values do not rescue the estimator's unconditional
performance, but they show that the central problem is concentrated in a
numerical tail rather than present in a typical replication.

\subsection{Native targets versus project targets}

For procedure $p$, the common-target error decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
\widehat\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}}
=(\widehat\theta_p-\theta_p)
+(\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}}). \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
The first term is native estimation error.  The second is an estimand gap that
exists before the panel is sampled.  Figure~\ref{fig:gaps} plots the mean of
the second term, native target minus project target.  Subtraction is used
because it isolates definition disagreement; it is not subtracting an
estimate from an arbitrary ``native firm variance.''

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{reports/dgp_estimator_matrix_cluster_v2/figures/native-project-target-gaps.pdf}
\caption{Population estimand differences averaged across the 100 populations
in each DGP.  The display is needed because common-target RMSE otherwise mixes
estimation error with disagreement about the target itself.}
\label{fig:gaps}
\end{figure}

KSS native $\QF$ gaps are approximately $0,-0.361,-0.778,-1.093,-0.693$ in
the AKM, Crippa, BLM, low-rank, and GKLP rows.  Yet KSS's native $\QF$ bias is
between $-0.00053$ and $0.00061$ in those DGPs.  Thus most KSS common-target
error under nonadditivity is not failure to estimate its additive projection;
it is that the additive projection omits interaction variation.  Under
independent assignment, Equation~\eqref{eq:qdecomp} gives the exact gap
$\Var_q(b_j)-\QF=-\HF/2\le0$.  Assignment is sorted here, so no universal sign
theorem applies; the negative simulated signs are features of the declared
sorting design.

BS20 native covariance exceeds $\Cassign$ by $0.414$ to $0.805$ across the
five DGPs, while its native covariance RMSE ranges only from $0.00867$ to
$0.02960$.  In the additive case, define the conditional-expectation operator
$Tb(i)=\E[b_J\mid I=i]$ and its adjoint
$T^*a(j)=\E[a_I\mid J=j]$.  Expanding Equation~\eqref{eq:bsnative} yields
\begin{equation}
\CBS-\Cassign
=\lVert Tb\rVert_p^2+\lVert T^*a\rVert_q^2
+\langle Tb,TT^*a\rangle_p\ge0. \label{eq:bsgap}
\end{equation}
The inequality follows because conditional expectation is a contraction and
$x^2+y^2-xy\ge0$.  This derivation explains the positive AKM gap.  Under a
general nonadditive schedule there is no corresponding universal sign result.

proposal discussion.  This table is intentionally separate from the common
project-target heatmap: it shows that BS20 estimates $\rhoBS$ and $\CBS$
precisely without implying that either equals $\Cassign$.

\subsection{Numerical warnings and squared-error tails}

Figure~\ref{fig:tails} compares all-returned project RMSE with RMSE conditional
on a diagnostic pass.  It is included because RMSE squares errors and can be
dominated by a few extreme completions.  The pass-only line is diagnostic; it
does not replace unconditional performance.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.90\textwidth]{reports/dgp_estimator_matrix_cluster_v2/figures/project-returned-vs-stable-rmse.pdf}
\caption{Project plug-in RMSE among all returned values and conditional on a
numerical pass.  The logarithmic scale shows that warning fits, rather than
ordinary sampling noise, generate the large unconditional errors.}
\label{fig:tails}
\end{figure}

For $\QF$, median absolute errors are only 0.0114 under Crippa, 0.0105 under
BLM, and 0.0159 under continuous low rank.  Their maximum absolute errors are
480, 498, and 230.  The five largest replications account for 91.1\%, 100\%,
and 96.9\% of total $\QF$ squared error in those three DGPs.  Thus a statement
such as ``the project estimator is usually close'' is true but incomplete;
unconditional risk is poor because the tail is severe.

The recorded local Gram diagnostics connect the tail to weak conditional
factor regressions.  Under continuous low rank, the median maximum condition
number is 42 among 93 passing fits but 84,006 among seven warnings; the median
maximum factor norm rises from 4.32 to 184.9.  Under BLM, the corresponding
condition numbers are 157 and 6,340.  Crippa contains both mild and severe
warnings: its warning median condition number is 117, close to the passing
median of 98, but its warning 90th percentile is 2.53 million and its warning
90th-percentile maximum factor norm is 154.  The diagnostic therefore flags a
continuum: some fits barely miss the reproducibility cutoff, while a smaller
ill-conditioned subset drives the enormous RMSE.

This evidence supplies a mathematical mechanism.  At rank $r$, each worker's
ALS update regresses on $[1,v_j']$ for the firms that worker visits.  Distinct
firms can nevertheless have nearly collinear factor vectors.  A nearly
singular local Gram matrix makes its pseudoinverse amplify small match-mean
noise into large worker or firm factors; completing unobserved matches then
propagates those extremes across the schedule.  More periods help, but
centering only changes normalization and cannot add independent local factor
variation.

\section{Interpretation}

The simulation delivers four conclusions in a linear chain.

First, the DGP-by-estimator matrix is informative precisely because the DGP is
separate from the estimator.  KSS is highly accurate under additive AKM, and
BLM is accurate under the discrete BLM DGP.  Applying them elsewhere reveals
the cost of their restrictions rather than re-confirming their assumptions by
construction.

Second, native and project targets must remain separate.  KSS accurately
estimates an additive projection even when that projection omits nonadditive
firm-side variation.  BS20 accurately estimates $\CBS$, which is not
$\Cassign$.  BLM accurately estimates its discrete schedule under the BLM DGP
but necessarily compresses continuous DGPs.  A single common-target RMSE is
useful only after these mappings and gaps are explicit.

Third, rank selection succeeds completely in the production design.  The
project's remaining problem is not that BIC erases Crippa's interaction or the
second low-rank factor.  It is that an unregularized continuous-factor
completion can be locally ill-conditioned even at the correct rank.

Fourth, the current plug-in is not the final LOO procedure.  Before a
leave-worker-out correction can be interpreted as solving the main remaining
bias, the completion stage needs a principled safeguard such as regularized
local regressions, conditioning-aware support, or a direct estimator of the
target functionals.  Any such change must be declared as part of the estimator
and re-evaluated without changing the DGP.  The present results therefore serve
as a transparent baseline: they show both the promise of the low-rank estimand
and the numerical limitation that a completed LOO estimator must address.
