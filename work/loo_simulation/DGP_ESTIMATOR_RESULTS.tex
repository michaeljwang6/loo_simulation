\begin{abstract}
This document archives a small simulation that applied four
procedures---Kline--Saggio--S\o lvsten (KSS),
Bonhomme--Lamadon--Manresa (BLM), Borovi\v{c}kov\'{a}--Shimer (BS20), and the
current project low-rank plug-in---to panels generated from five distinct
data-generating processes (DGPs).  A DGP is the rule that creates the full
worker--firm wage schedule, the assignment probabilities, and the sampled
panel.  The project leave-one-out (LOO) correction
is still under development and is not simulated.  The central result is that
three distinct issues must be kept separate: sampling error, disagreement
between population estimands, and whether an estimator's required support is
present. A forensic audit showed that the original BLM summaries mixed
supported fits with fits missing stayer-class support. The counts below apply
that support reclassification, but they still use the wrong full-history
mover/stayer construction. They are historical diagnostics, not evidence on
the corrected two-period BLM procedure. Corrected numerical results will be
added only after the declared cluster run is complete.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}

The experiment asks one symmetric question: what happens when every procedure
is used under every DGP?  This is more informative than generating all panels
from an additive Abowd--Kramarz--Margolis (AKM) model because an additive design
mechanically favors an additive estimator.  The five DGPs are additive AKM, Crippa/Tukey
nonadditivity, discrete BLM types, continuous low-rank factors, and
Gibbons--Katz--Lemieux--Parent (GKLP) comparative advantage.  Each DGP has 100
independent Monte Carlo replications.
One Monte Carlo replication means drawing a new finite population, sampling one
panel from it, running every procedure, and comparing each returned number with
that population's known truth.

The project procedure in this document is the current Bayesian information
criterion (BIC)-selected low-rank plug-in.  It is not called the final project
LOO estimator because the full LOO
correction has not yet been implemented.

The argument proceeds in the order needed to interpret a simulation result.
Section 2 defines the four economic quantities to be recovered.  Section 3
states every DGP parameter.  Section 4 explains what each procedure actually
returns and which project quantities, if any, can be constructed from it.
Section 5 defines feasibility, bias, and root mean squared error.  The results
then ask, in order: can the procedure be run, does it work in its own benchmark,
how accurate is it for a common target, are native and project targets different,
does rank selection recover the interaction dimension, and do warning fits
create tail risk.  This order prevents a low error computed on a selected
subset from being mistaken for overall good performance.

Every reported number is calculated from the support-audited merged output.
Its configuration fingerprint is
\texttt{3e8ab3b57d35190989c47117f139b414b9f656db8a933d6c5d1df6d2aa5fbcc4}.
The audit reconstructs panels and estimated BLM firm classes from the saved
seeds, but does not refit a successful estimator.  Statements borrowed from a
paper or software specification are cited where used; otherwise, equations are
definitions or explicit derivations.

The decomposition is unique after defining
\begin{align}
\mu&=\E_{pq}[m_{ij}],
&a_i&=\E_q[m_{ij}\mid i]-\mu,\\
b_j&=\E_p[m_{ij}\mid j]-\mu,
&h_{ij}&=m_{ij}-\mu-a_i-b_j. \label{eq:anova}
\end{align}
Thus the $p$-weighted mean of $a_i$, the $q$-weighted mean of $b_j$, and both
weighted margins of $h_{ij}$ are zero.  Consequently,
\begin{align}
m_{ij}-\E_q[m_{ij}\mid i] &= b_j+h_{ij}, \\
\QF &= \Var_q(b_j)+\E_{pq}[h_{ij}^2]
      =\Var_q(b_j)+\frac{\HF}{2}. \label{eq:q-decomp}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:q-decomp} is a direct derivation from the project definitions,
not an assumption about an estimator.

\subsection{Why these four project quantities are reported}

The four quantities answer different economic questions and are therefore not
interchangeable.  $\QF$ asks how much the systematic wage schedule changes on
the firm side after holding the worker fixed.  $\HF$ asks how much of the
schedule is genuinely nonadditive, meaning that firm differences depend on the
worker.  The factor 2 in $\HF$ is chosen so that the accounting identity in
Equation~\eqref{eq:q-decomp} has the simple term $\HF/2$.  $\rhoH$ divides the
interaction contribution $\HF/2$ by total firm-side variation $\QF$; it is a
unit-free interaction share and is easier to compare across DGPs of different
scale.  Finally, $\Cassign$ asks how much observed sorting changes schedule
variance relative to random matching with the same worker and firm marginals.
The factor $1/2$ makes $\Cassign=\Cov_P(a_i,b_j)$ in the additive case.

Together the four objects distinguish the amount of firm-side heterogeneity
($\QF$), its nonadditive part ($\HF$ and $\rhoH$), and the assignment channel
($\Cassign$).  Worker-side analogues are omitted because the project question
in this experiment is explicitly firm-side.  $\QF$, $\HF$, and $\Cassign$ are
measured in squared wage units; $\rhoH$ is unitless.

\section{Data-generating processes}

\subsection{Common population and panel design}

Every population contains 300 workers and 18 firms.  Every panel contains ten
periods, hence 3,000 observations before procedure-specific cleaning.  The
systematic wage schedule has grand mean zero.  Worker and firm population
marginals are uniform.  The observation disturbance is independent and
normally distributed with mean zero and standard deviation 0.5.

Because this is a simulation, the complete $300\times18$ schedule and all
$P_{ij}$ are known before the panel is sampled.  The population truths in
Section 2 are therefore computed by exact finite sums over all 5,400
worker--firm cells.  They are not estimated from the simulated panel.  The
base random seed is 20260804, so every population and panel is reproducible.

All Gaussian coordinates are redrawn in every replication.  Main-effect
vectors are standardized to finite-population mean zero and variance one.
Factor columns are centered and orthonormalized under the uniform population
weights: each column has weighted mean zero and weighted variance one, and
distinct columns have weighted covariance zero.  A singular value below is the
strength of one worker-factor--firm-factor interaction direction.  The stated
singular values therefore hold exactly in every realized population, rather
than only in expectation.

The rank of an interaction is the number of nonzero worker-factor--firm-factor
directions: rank zero is additive, rank one has one such direction, and so on.
In Table~\ref{tab:dgps}, $U_i$ and $V_j$ denote row vectors of normalized factor
scores, and a prime denotes transposition.  $N(0,1)$ denotes a normal draw with
mean zero and variance one before the finite-population normalization.

Observed matching probabilities are defined by
\begin{equation}
P_{ij}=R_iS_j\exp\!\left\{0.5a_ib_j
  +0.3\frac{h_{ij}}{\sqrt{\E_{pq}[h_{ij}^2]}}\right\},
\label{eq:assignment}
\end{equation}
where $a_i,b_j,h_{ij}$ are the realized components of the relevant DGP and the
positive balancing constants $R_i$ and $S_j$ restore uniform worker and firm
marginals.  For AKM, $h_{ij}=0$ and the second sorting term is omitted.  Thus
the coefficient 0.5 controls sorting on common worker and firm components,
while 0.3 controls sorting on match-specific gains.  Define a worker's
conditional firm probabilities by $\pi_{ij}=P_{ij}/p_i$.  The first firm is
drawn from $\pi_i$.  In each later period, the worker makes an independent draw
from $\pi_i$ with probability 0.40 and otherwise remains at the current firm.
Because the new draw can equal the current firm, a redraw need not create an
observed move.

\section{Procedures}

\subsection{KSS}

KSS applies leave-out variance-component corrections to a linear model with
many effects and unrestricted heteroskedasticity \cite{kss2020}.  Here PyTwoWay
0.3.21 supplies the heteroskedastic KSS correction with approximate trace and
leverage calculations (\texttt{fe\_exact=False}) \cite{pytwoway}.  Its native
population target is the best assignment-weighted additive approximation:
\begin{equation}
(\mu,\alpha,\psi)
=\arg\min_{\mu,\alpha,\psi}
\E_P[(m_{ij}-\mu-\alpha_i-\psi_j)^2].
\label{eq:kss-projection}
\end{equation}
This target equals the true schedule under AKM.  Under a nonadditive DGP it is
``pseudo-true'': it is the closest additive schedule, not the complete schedule.
KSS natively reports $\Var_q(\psi_j)$ and $\Cov_P(\alpha_i,\psi_j)$.

The code calculates three versions.  AKM-FE is the uncorrected fixed-effect
plug-in.  KSS-HO applies the homoskedastic leave-out correction, which assumes
one error variance.  KSS-HE allows observation-specific error variances and is
the main KSS specification graphed below.  Showing one corrected KSS column
keeps the DGP-by-procedure comparison readable; KSS-HE is chosen because it
maintains the weaker error-variance assumption.  The AKM-FE and KSS-HO outputs
remain in the machine-readable results.

Before estimation, PyTwoWay keeps a leave-one-spell-connected sample: the
worker--firm network remains connected after any single continuous employment
spell is removed.  It collapses observations when computing that network,
drops workers observed at only one firm for only one spell, and retains return
spells.  This cleaning is procedure-specific; the DGP itself is unchanged.

The additive restriction also implies
\begin{equation}
h_{ij}^{\mathrm{KSS}}=0,\qquad
\HF^{\mathrm{KSS}}=0,\qquad
\rhoH^{\mathrm{KSS}}=0\quad\text{if }\QF^{\mathrm{KSS}}>0.
\label{eq:kss-zero}
\end{equation}
These are imposed zeros, not interaction quantities estimated from the data.
The corrected report includes them to show exactly what additivity implies.
KSS residual variance is not relabeled as $\HF$: under misspecification, that
residual mixes observation noise with omitted schedule structure.

\subsection{BLM}

BLM models a discrete worker type by firm class wage distribution and estimates
firm classes before the finite-mixture wage model \cite{blm2019}.  A wage CDF
is the cumulative distribution function of wages; its value at a threshold is
the share of observations at or below that threshold.  Every DGP is fit using
two worker types, three estimated firm classes, ten CDF evaluation points, four
random initializations, the best two retained starts, at most 250 mover and
stayer iterations, and convergence threshold $10^{-6}$.  Multiple starts are
used because a finite-mixture likelihood can have several local optima.  The
implementation is PyTwoWay 0.3.21 \cite{pytwoway}.

The archived tables used the following pipeline. It is recorded so that the
old results are reproducible, not because it is the current specification:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Drop every worker who leaves a firm and later returns to it.
\item Collapse the remaining panel to employment spells.
\item Estimate three firm clusters from wage CDF features using K-means.
\item Convert the spell panel to mover and stayer event-study observations.
\item Require all three stayer firm classes and all nine mover origin--destination class pairs.
\item Fit four mover-model starts and retain the two best likelihoods.
\item Among the two highest-likelihood starts, select the one with the highest
minimum mover-graph connectedness across worker types.
\item Fit the stayer model conditional on the selected mover model.
\end{enumerate}

An employment spell is a worker's uninterrupted run at one firm.  A mover
event joins the worker's origin and destination spells; a stayer event records
a worker observed at one firm throughout. That full-history definition is the
error: it is stricter than the static BLM definition and turns workers who move
after the comparison pair into movers. K-means is the clustering algorithm
that groups firms with similar wage-CDF features.

The corrected pipeline separates clustering from construction of the BLM
estimation sample. First, it estimates the three firm classes from wage CDFs
using all ten periods. Second, it keeps one record for each worker observed in
periods 0 and 1, with the worker's actual wages and firm classes in those two
periods. Third, it defines $m_i=1\{j_{i0}\ne j_{i1}\}$ and splits this single
two-period table into movers and stayers. It does not drop returners and does
not collapse the ten-period history into spells. Finally, it applies the same
three-class and nine-class-pair support check and then fits the same PyTwoWay
model. Thus later periods inform firm clustering but cannot change worker
$i$'s mover/stayer label for the static likelihood.

The support check is a separate correction retained in the new pipeline.
PyTwoWay 0.3.21 reshapes probability counts to
$2\times 3^2$ for movers and $2\times3$ for stayers without padding absent
categories.  Without a preflight support check, a missing terminal label raises
a reshape exception, while a missing interior label can instead create a zero
probability row.  The corrected code detects the common economic cause before
calling the optimizer.

BLM's worker types and firm classes are latent, so their numerical labels have
no intrinsic order.  For simulation scoring only, the estimated $2\times3$
cell-mean table is relabeled by the worker- and firm-label permutation that
minimizes cell-mean squared error relative to a known evaluation table.  Under
the grouped BLM DGP, that evaluation table uses the simulated true types.  For
the other DGPs, workers and firms are placed into deterministic equal-count bins
ordered by their product-marginal mean wages.  These truth-based (``oracle'')
labels are never given to the BLM estimator.

The four project functionals are then applied to the aligned estimated cell
means using the known population probability mass of each evaluation cell.
This use of population cell weights is another simulation-only scoring choice:
it isolates error in BLM's wage cell means rather than also asking BLM to
estimate the assignment law.  Finally, those grouped functionals are compared
with the functionals of the full $300\times18$ project schedule.  Therefore the
reported BLM error outside its discrete DGP includes the deliberate loss from
compressing a continuous schedule into six cells.

\subsection{BS20}

BS20 estimates moments of worker and firm wage types in matched data
\cite{bs2020}.  PyTwoWay's weighted standard estimator is used after
strongly-connected, spell-level, no-return cleaning \cite{pytwoway}.  The native
population covariance is
\begin{equation}
\CBS=\Cov_P(\lambda_i,\mu_j),\qquad
\lambda_i=\E_P[m_{ij}\mid i],\quad
\mu_j=\E_P[m_{ij}\mid j].
\label{eq:bs-native}
\end{equation}
The code reads PyTwoWay's \texttt{cov(lambda, mu)} directly.  It does not
construct that number from project quantities.  Because $\CBS$ is not generally
$\Cassign$, BS20 is no longer displayed as an estimator of $\Cassign$.  Their
population difference is reported separately as an estimand comparison.

Concretely, ``strongly connected'' means every retained firm can be reached
from every other through directed worker moves.  The panel is collapsed to one
row per continuous employment spell, return spells are removed, and each
retained worker and firm must appear in at least two spell rows.  These choices
follow the configured PyTwoWay BS20 pipeline and are not imposed on the other
procedures.

\subsection{Project low-rank plug-in}

The current project fit is
\begin{equation}
m_{ij}=\alpha_i+\psi_j+
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}s_{\ell}U_{i\ell}V_{j\ell}.
\label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
Here $U_{i\ell}$ and $V_{j\ell}$ are normalized worker and firm factor scores,
$s_\ell$ is the strength of factor $\ell$, and $r$ is the number of retained
interaction directions.
Positive ranks use three starts, tolerance $10^{-6}$, and at most 300
iterations.  Candidate ranks are $\{0,1\}$ for AKM and grouped BLM and
$\{0,1,2\}$ for Crippa, continuous low-rank, and GKLP.  All candidates use a
common retained core: minimum degree 3 for $\{0,1\}$ and degree 4 for
$\{0,1,2\}$.

The selected rank minimizes the Bayesian information criterion (BIC)
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{BIC}(r)
=n\log(\operatorname{SSE}_r/n)+\operatorname{df}(r)\log n,
\qquad
\operatorname{df}(r)=N+J-1+r(N+J-2-r).
\label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:bic} is an exploratory simulation rule.  It is not part of
the unfinished project LOO theory.  Here $\operatorname{SSE}_r$ is the weighted
sum of squared observed match-mean residuals at rank $r$, $n$ is the retained
number of wage observations, and $N$ and $J$ are the retained numbers of
workers and firms.  The first term rewards fit; the degrees-of-freedom penalty
discourages extra interaction dimensions.

The plug-in first retains a connected worker--firm core with the stated minimum
degree, meaning that every retained worker and firm has at least three or four
distinct observed partners as specified above.  It estimates observed match
means and completes the unobserved entries of the schedule using
Equation~\eqref{eq:lowrank}.  It then applies the definitions
in Section 2 to the completed schedule and the empirical assignment weights in
that retained core.  Comparing those estimates with the full-population truth
therefore includes sampling, core selection, completion, and plug-in error.

\section{Evaluation rules}

Each procedure is run once in each of 100 independent replications of each
DGP.  For a returned estimate $\widehat\theta_s$ and its replication-specific
population truth $\theta_s$, let $S$ be the number of admissible returned
estimates in the row being summarized and define
\begin{align}
e_s&=\widehat\theta_s-\theta_s,\\
\operatorname{Bias}&=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S e_s,
&\operatorname{RMSE}&=\sqrt{\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^S e_s^2}.
\label{eq:performance}
\end{align}
Bias answers whether errors point systematically upward or downward.  RMSE
combines systematic error and dispersion in one number and penalizes rare large
errors, so it is the primary accuracy metric in the cross-procedure graph.
Because the four economic quantities have different scales, RMSE values should
be compared across procedures within a metric panel, not across different
metric panels.  Bias is reported where its sign is economically informative,
rather than adding a second large heatmap that would obscure the main comparison.

Before calculating either accuracy measure, the experiment records whether a
procedure could legitimately return an estimate.  This is necessary because a
small RMSE on a selected minority of panels does not imply that a procedure is
generally feasible.  The experiment separates four statuses:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pass/completed.} KSS and BS20 returned a value.  BLM additionally
has complete support and no one-step likelihood decline greater than $10^{-4}$.
The project fit satisfies convergence and multi-start diagnostics.
\item \textbf{Returned with warning.} An admissible BLM or project fit returned
finite values but failed its procedure-specific numerical diagnostic.
\item \textbf{Unsupported.} BLM lacks a stayer class or mover class-pair after
its own cleaning and clustering.  It is not sent to the optimizer.
\item \textbf{Estimator failure.} An admissible sample reaches the estimator,
but the estimator returns no value.
\end{itemize}

The $10^{-4}$ BLM likelihood threshold is an engineering diagnostic, not a
statistical critical value.  It is 100 times the $10^{-6}$ optimizer tolerance:
the wider band ignores rounding-scale movement while flagging a meaningful
decrease in a likelihood that should improve during the update.  For a
positive-rank project fit, at least two of
three starts must have weighted squared-error objectives within
$0.0001\max(1,O^*)$ of the best objective $O^*$, and their $\QF$, $\HF$, and
$\Cassign$ spreads must each be within
$0.001\max(1,|\theta^*|)$, where $\theta^*$ is the corresponding quantity from
the best start.  These thresholds are also engineering checks.  A
second near-best start is required so that the solution is not supported by a
single favorable initialization.  Scaling by $\max(1,|\theta^*|)$ combines an
absolute tolerance near zero with a relative tolerance for large quantities;
0.001 means agreement within one thousandth on that scale.  A pass does not
prove correct specification, correct rank, or low bias.

RMSE uses all values returned from admissible samples.  Unsupported samples do
not enter the RMSE because no estimate exists.  Here ``admissible returned''
means pass plus returned-with-warning.  A pass-only RMSE is reported separately
as a numerical diagnostic and is not treated as unconditional procedure
performance.

tables support precise lookup, while the graphs make patterns across the full
DGP-by-procedure matrix visible at a glance.

The BLM audit finds 257 unsupported samples and zero estimator failures.  The
unsupported samples consist of 3 with no stayers, 246 with at least one absent
stayer class, 6 with only a missing mover class-pair, and 2 with both a missing
stayer class and mover class-pair.  Thus 243 samples have complete support; 242
pass and one returns with a likelihood-path warning.

The high unsupported rate follows from the panel design rather than random-start
failure.  To see the mechanism, consider the uniform-choice benchmark in which
a worker chooses each of 18 firms with probability $1/18$.  The probability of
no actual firm change in a later period is then
\begin{equation}
0.6+\frac{0.4}{18}=0.622.
\end{equation}
The probability of remaining at the same firm through nine later periods is
$0.622^9=0.014$, or about four workers out of 300.  Sorting makes firm choices
worker-specific, so this is an explanatory benchmark rather than the exact
probability in Equation~\eqref{eq:assignment}.  After returners are dropped,
the realized stayers must cover all three firm classes.  The reported
classification always uses the exact cleaned-panel support, not this benchmark.

\subsection{Native targets versus project targets}

For procedure $p$, comparison with the project truth decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\widehat\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}}
=\underbrace{(\widehat\theta_p-\theta_p)}_{\text{estimation error}}
+\underbrace{(\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}})}_{\text{estimand difference}}.
\label{eq:target-gap}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:gaps} and Table~\ref{tab:gaps} report the second term averaged
over all 100 populations.  They contain no estimator sampling error.  The table
gives exact averages; the dot plot is included to make the sign and relative
size of the three kinds of target disagreement immediately visible.  The three
plotted contrasts are the direct native moments returned by KSS-HE and BS20.
BLM's four grouped-schedule gaps are discussed separately below because they
measure discretization into evaluation cells rather than a native scalar named
by BLM; the project plug-in has no gap because its target is the project truth
by definition.

The negative KSS firm-variance differences have an exact explanation under
independent assignment.  The additive projection then recovers $b_j$, so
Equation~\eqref{eq:q-decomp} gives
\begin{equation}
\Var_q(\psi_j)-\QF=-\frac{\HF}{2}\leq0.
\label{eq:kss-gap}
\end{equation}
With sorted assignment, the pseudo-true additive firm effect can absorb part of
$h_{ij}$, so Equation~\eqref{eq:kss-gap} no longer fixes the sign.  The negative
values in the four sorted nonadditive designs are therefore informative
simulation results, not a universal theorem.  The KSS covariance gaps are small
and of mixed sign, confirming that no analogous sign claim is warranted.

The BS20 object also needs its own explanation.  Under an additive schedule
$m_{ij}=a_i+b_j$, define the conditional-expectation operators
$Tb(i)=\E[b_J\mid I=i]$ and $T^*a(j)=\E[a_I\mid J=j]$.  Then
\begin{align}
\lambda_i &= a_i+Tb(i), & \mu_j &= b_j+T^*a(j), \\
\CBS-\Cassign
&=\lVert Tb\rVert^2+\lVert T^*a\rVert^2
  +\langle Tb,TT^*a\rangle \geq 0. \label{eq:bs-gap}
\end{align}
Here $T$ averages a firm quantity over the firms assigned to a worker, $T^*$
averages a worker quantity over the workers assigned to a firm,
$\lVert x\rVert^2$ is the relevant marginally weighted mean of $x^2$, and
$\langle x,z\rangle$ is the corresponding weighted cross-product.  To see the
sign, Cauchy--Schwarz and the mean-square contraction property of conditional
expectation give
$\langle Tb,TT^*a\rangle\geq-\lVert Tb\rVert\lVert T^*a\rVert$.
The right side of Equation~\eqref{eq:bs-gap} is therefore at least
$x^2+y^2-xy\geq0$, with $x=\lVert Tb\rVert$ and
$y=\lVert T^*a\rVert$.  Thus the positive AKM gap has a mathematical
explanation: sorting enters both conditional wage types.  For a
general nonadditive schedule, Equation~\eqref{eq:bs-gap} need not hold.  The
positive gaps in the other four rows are features of these DGPs, not a general
property imposed by the code.

BLM has another population difference outside its discrete DGP.  Replacing a
continuous wage surface with a $2\times3$ grouped surface changes the project
functionals even before estimation.  The grouped-minus-full $\HF$ gaps are
$-0.591$ for Crippa, $-2.452$ for continuous low rank, and $-1.959$ for GKLP.
The grouped BLM DGP has zero gap up to floating-point error because its true
labels already define the scoring cells.

Crippa makes the distinction sharp: every project fit passes numerically, but
BIC selects rank zero in every replication.  The estimator therefore sets the

and discarding the weaker singular-value-0.5 direction.  A future LOO bias
correction cannot repair an interaction dimension removed by rank selection.

\subsection{Warnings and tail risk}

Figure~\ref{fig:tail} compares project RMSE among all returned fits with RMSE
conditional on passing the numerical diagnostics.  A large gap shows that a
small warning subset dominates squared error.  The pass-only line is useful for
diagnosis, but it conditions on an estimation outcome and cannot replace
all-returned performance.  AKM is omitted because it has no project warnings;
Crippa is retained as a reference case in which the two lines coincide.  A
logarithmic vertical axis is necessary because the warning fits make some RMSEs
millions of times larger than the pass-only values.

\begin{figure}[H]

The lines coincide for Crippa because all 100 fits pass.  In the grouped BLM
DGP, by contrast, 94 warning fits raise the all-returned $\QF$, $\HF$, and
$\Cassign$ RMSEs from ordinary values below two to roughly $10^6$.  The 22
low-rank warnings and six GKLP warnings likewise raise those three squared-wage
metrics by several orders of magnitude.  The much smaller gap for $\rhoH$ does
not make the underlying schedules stable; it reflects the normalization of
interaction variation by total firm-side variation.  Thus the warning label
identifies economically consequential tail risk rather than a cosmetic
convergence message.

\section{Interpretation and next simulation changes}

The full DGP-by-estimator matrix answers the original question more clearly
than an all-AKM exercise.  KSS works in its additive benchmark, and BLM works
in its discrete benchmark when its support requirements are met.  Outside
those benchmarks, performance changes for two different reasons: estimators
can be inaccurate for their native target, and native targets can differ from
the project target.  Equation~\eqref{eq:target-gap} shows why those sources must
not be combined without explanation.

The archived BLM result is not evidence that the PyTwoWay optimizer fails half
the time. The full-history conversion incorrectly left only permanent stayers
in the stayer sample, so the small design often lacked a stayer class. The
corrected estimator sample uses periods 0 and 1 and leaves later moves out of
that label. The replacement DGP has 25,000 workers, 5,000 firms, and mechanical
retention probability 0.8, so approximately 20,000 workers are mechanically
retained at their period-0 firm and approximately 5,000 redraw in period 1
before allowing for redraws back to the same firm. These are declared DGP
parameters; they are not observations deleted or manufactured by the
estimator. The resulting accuracy and support rates remain empirical questions
until all 50 shards have completed.

For the project procedure, the dominant present issue is rank selection and
positive-rank reproducibility, not only small-sample plug-in bias.  Crippa shows
complete under-selection with numerical convergence; the rank-two design shows
systematic truncation; and the warning fits create very large squared-error
tails.  Rank choice and stable schedule completion therefore need to be solved
before a leave-out correction can be interpreted as the principal remaining
improvement.

Finally, the reporting distinctions are substantive.  KSS's interaction zeros
are now labeled ``imposed,'' BS20 is reported under its native covariance rather
than under $\Cassign$, and BLM support failures are separate from optimizer
failures.  These labels prevent a numerical table from silently changing the
meaning of an estimand.
