\section{Question, notation, and estimands}

The proposal's wage interface is
\begin{equation}
Y_{ij}=X_{ij}'\delta+\alpha_i+\psi_j+u_i'\Lambda v_j+
\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\Lambda)=r.
\label{eq:proposal-model}
\end{equation}
Simulation implementation:
\begin{itemize}
\item Set $X_{ij}'\delta=0$ to isolate worker, firm, and interaction terms.
\item Define the systematic schedule as $m_{ij}=\E[Y_{ij}\mid i,j]$.
\item Let $J_{it}$ be worker $i$'s employer in period $t$ and observe
$Y_{it}=m_{iJ_{it}}+\varepsilon_{it}$.
\item Apply every procedure under every DGP.  An additive-only design would
favor additive methods by construction.
\item In each replication, draw a population and panel, run all procedures,
and score them against that population's exact truth.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:notation} defines every symbol used below.  A prime denotes
transposition.  All expectations and variances are finite sums, not
large-population approximations.

For any completed schedule, use the product-weighted ANOVA decomposition
\begin{align}
\mu&=\E_{pq}[m_{ij}],
&a_i&=\E_q[m_{ij}\mid i]-\mu,\\
b_j&=\E_p[m_{ij}\mid j]-\mu,
&h_{ij}&=m_{ij}-\mu-a_i-b_j. \label{eq:anova}
\end{align}
The weighted margins of $h_{ij}$ are zero.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\QF=\Var_q(b_j)+\E_{pq}[h_{ij}^2]
=\Var_q(b_j)+\frac{\HF}{2}. \label{eq:qdecomp}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:qdecomp} follows by substitution into the definition of
$\QF$ and the zero weighted margins of $h_{ij}$.  In a centered low-rank DGP,
$(a_i,b_j,h_{ij})$ correspond to
$(\alpha_i,\psi_j,u_i'\Lambda v_j)$; they do not replace the proposal symbols.

\textbf{Interpretation.} $\QF$ measures all firm-side schedule variation;
$\HF$ isolates the part that changes with the worker; $\rhoH$ is its unit-free
share; $\Cassign$ is the variance change from observed rather than independent
matching, holding worker and firm marginals fixed.

\section{Simulation design}

\subsection{Common population, assignment, and panel}

Fixed settings:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Size:} $N=25{,}000$ workers, $J=5{,}000$ firms, $T=60$ periods;
1.5 million observations before procedure-specific cleaning.
\item \textbf{Why 25,000 workers:} the lower end of the proposed
25,000--50,000 range keeps cluster cost feasible and gives five workers per
firm on average.  The 5,000-firm count was fixed in advance.
\item \textbf{Weights:} uniform worker and firm population marginals.
\item \textbf{Noise:} independent
$\varepsilon_{it}\sim N(0,0.5^2)$.  The standard deviation is one half of the
standardized main-effect standard deviation.
\item \textbf{Seeds:} master seed 20260812; separately derived population,
panel, and estimator seeds by replication.
\item \textbf{Normalization:} Gaussian main effects have finite-population
mean zero and variance one.  Factor columns are centered and weighted
orthonormal.  Reported singular values therefore hold in each population.
\end{itemize}

Observed assignment probabilities are
\begin{equation}
P^{\mathrm{obs}}_{ij}=R_iS_j\exp\!\left\{0.5a_ib_j
+0.3\frac{h_{ij}}{\sqrt{\E_{pq}[h_{ij}^2]}}\right\},
\label{eq:assignment}
\end{equation}
where the interaction term is omitted when $h_{ij}=0$.  Interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
\item $0.5$ controls sorting on common worker and firm components.
\item $0.3$ controls sorting on match-specific gains.
\item Positive $R_i,S_j$ restore uniform marginals.  Thus $\Cassign$ changes
only with the joint matching pattern, not with worker or firm composition.
\item The coefficients create visible sorting without nearly deterministic
matching.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Mobility.} Let $\pi_{ij}=P^{\mathrm{obs}}_{ij}/p_i$.  Draw the initial
firm from $\pi_i$.  Later, stay with probability 0.6 or redraw from $\pi_i$
with probability 0.4.  A redraw may return the same firm, so realized retention
is slightly above 0.6.  This gives about as many redraw opportunities as 120
periods with 0.8 retention, using a shorter and more mobile panel.

\textbf{Design lock and cluster run.} A one-replication mobility gate verified
full-population retention and a numerical pass for rank two.  The 60-period
design was then fixed before the 100-replication accuracy results were viewed.
The run used 50 independent shards; the merge checked the fingerprint,
replication indices, and duplicates.  One hundred replications balance cost
with detection of the rare failures missed by a small pilot.

\textbf{Exact truth.} Each complete $25{,}000\times5{,}000$ schedule and
assignment matrix is known.  Project truths use all 125 million worker--firm
cells before panel sampling; they are not estimates from observed matches.

Underlying draws:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{AKM and Crippa/Tukey:} independent raw worker and firm effects
from $N(0,1)$.
\item \textbf{Continuous low rank:} raw factors
$\widetilde u_{i\ell}=0.35\widetilde\alpha_i+
\sqrt{1-0.35^2}\,z_{i\ell}$ and
$\widetilde v_{j\ell}=0.25\widetilde\psi_j+
\sqrt{1-0.25^2}\,z_{j\ell}$; innovations are standard normal before
normalization.
\item \textbf{GKLP:} $(Z_i,\eta_i)$ and $(c_j,b_j)$ are jointly standard
Gaussian with correlations 0.35 and 0.25.
\item \textbf{BLM:} worker and firm labels are as evenly sized as possible,
then shuffled.  Type main effects and raw type factors are independent
standard Gaussian draws before weighted normalization.  The cell table is
redrawn in every replication.
\end{itemize}

Purpose of each DGP:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{AKM:} additive implementation check.
\item \textbf{Crippa/Tukey:} simplest smooth rank-one nonadditivity.
\item \textbf{BLM:} exact specification for the grouped procedure.
\item \textbf{Low-rank factors:} direct rank-two implementation of the
proposal interface.
\item \textbf{GKLP:} economic comparative advantage; low rank but not built
from the project estimator.
\end{itemize}

The Crippa and GKLP functional forms are adapted from the cited papers.
Normalization, sorting coefficients, and distributions are simulation choices.
DGP code is separate from estimator code: no estimator receives latent truth
or the complete schedule.

\section{Procedures and evaluation rules}

\subsection{KSS-HE}

KSS corrects plug-in variance components in linear models with many fixed
effects; KSS-HE allows unrestricted heteroskedasticity \cite{kss2020}.  Its
native target is the assignment-weighted additive projection
\begin{equation}
(\mu,\alpha,\psi)=\arg\min_{\mu,\alpha,\psi}
\E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[(m_{ij}-\mu-\alpha_i-\psi_j)^2]. \label{eq:kssprojection}
\end{equation}
Implementation and mapping:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Software:} PyTwoWay 0.3.21 with approximate trace and leverage
calculations ($\texttt{fe\_exact=False}$) \cite{pytwoway}.  Exact leverage is
impractical at 1.5 million observations; the setting is fixed across DGPs.
\item \textbf{Native outputs:}
$V_{\psi}^{\mathrm{AKM}}=\Var_q(\psi_j^{\mathrm{AKM}})$ and
$C_{\psi\alpha}^{\mathrm{AKM}}=
\Cov_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}(\alpha_i^{\mathrm{AKM}},
\psi_j^{\mathrm{AKM}})$.
\item \textbf{Nonadditivity:} these native objects need not equal $\QF$ and
$\Cassign$.  KSS imposes $\HF=\rhoH=0$; these are structural zeros, not
estimated interaction moments.
\item \textbf{Displayed version:} KSS-HE, because it makes the weaker
error-variance assumption.  AKM-FE and KSS-HO remain in the CSV files.
\item \textbf{Cleaning:} leave-one-spell connected cleaning retains all 1.5
million observations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{BLM}

BLM uses discrete worker types and firm classes \cite{blm2019}.  This
implementation uses PyTwoWay 0.3.21 \cite{pytwoway}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Model size:} two worker types and three firm classes.  This is
correct under the BLM DGP and deliberately coarse under continuous DGPs.
\item \textbf{Firm clustering:} wage-CDF features use all 60 periods.
\item \textbf{Likelihood sample:} periods 0 and 1 only; stayer if
$j_{i0}=j_{i1}$, mover otherwise.  Later moves do not alter this label.  This
avoids the earlier error of restricting stayers to workers who never move in
60 periods.
\item \textbf{Optimization:} ten CDF points, four initializations, best two
retained starts, 250-iteration cap, and tolerance $10^{-6}$; fixed across DGPs.
\item \textbf{Support:} require all three stayer classes and nine mover
origin--destination pairs.  All 500 samples pass.  Each fit uses 50,000
static-period observations.
\item \textbf{Scoring:} after estimation, permute the $2\times3$ table to
minimize cell-mean squared error against a known evaluation table.  Under BLM,
use simulated groups; under continuous DGPs, use deterministic equal-count
groups ordered by product-marginal mean wage.  Outside the BLM DGP, error
against the full truth therefore includes six-cell approximation error.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{BS20}

BS20 estimates moments of worker and firm wage types \cite{bs2020}.  Its native
covariance is
\begin{equation}
\CBS=\Cov_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}(\lambda_i^{\mathrm{BS}},
\mu_j^{\mathrm{BS}}),\qquad
\rhoBS=\operatorname{Corr}_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}(\lambda_i^{\mathrm{BS}},
\mu_j^{\mathrm{BS}}), \label{eq:bsnative}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_i^{\mathrm{BS}}=\E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[m_{ij}\mid i]$ and
$\mu_j^{\mathrm{BS}}=\E_{P^{\mathrm{obs}}}[m_{ij}\mid j]$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Apply PyTwoWay's weighted estimator after strongly connected,
spell-level, no-return cleaning \cite{pytwoway}; about 1.491 million
observations remain on average.
\item Read \texttt{cov(lambda, mu)} directly.  It is not constructed from
project quantities.
\item Since $\CBS\ne\Cassign$ in general, score BS20 against $\rhoBS$ and
$\CBS$ and show the estimand gap.  Do not report it as an estimator of
$\Cassign$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Project low-rank plug-in}

The current project benchmark fits
\begin{equation}
m_{ij}=\alpha_i+\psi_j+u_i'\Lambda v_j,
\qquad \operatorname{rank}(\Lambda)=r. \label{eq:lowrank}
\end{equation}
Implementation:
\begin{itemize}
\item Collapse periods to worker--firm match means; weight by match counts.
\item Fit positive ranks by alternating least squares (ALS): six exploratory
starts, then three perturbed confirmation starts around the best basin.
\item Center and SVD-normalize after every ALS iteration.  Thus the numerical
tail below is not caused by omitted centering.
\item Use tolerance $10^{-6}$ and a 300-iteration cap.
\item At rank $r$, require $r+2$ distinct firm partners: one above the
algebraic $r+1$ minimum.  Use degree-three support for candidates $\{0,1\}$
and degree four for $\{0,1,2\}$.
\item Retain all 25,000 workers, 5,000 firms, and 1.5 million observations.
\end{itemize}

BIC selects the rank:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{BIC}(r)=n\log(\operatorname{SSE}_r/n)
+\operatorname{df}(r)\log n,
\qquad
\operatorname{df}(r)=N+J-1+r(N+J-2-r). \label{eq:bic}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
\item Candidates are $\{0,1\}$ under AKM and BLM and $\{0,1,2\}$ under
Crippa, continuous low rank, and GKLP.
\item Each set contains the DGP rank and an underfitted alternative.  Rank two
is impossible with two BLM worker types; ranks above two are outside scope.
\item Equation~\eqref{eq:bic} is a simulation choice, not a result from the
unfinished LOO theory.
\item The selected fit completes unobserved cells, then applies the project
definitions.  It is a plug-in without the leave-worker-out correction.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{``Conditioning as part of the estimator'':} before each update,

estimation; it does not modify the estimator.
\item \textbf{Required next experiment:} fix DGP draws and seeds; compare the
baseline with declared variants using unconditional bias, RMSE, warning rate,
and any support-induced change in the target population.
\item \textbf{Current evidence:} the variants are research directions, not
simulation findings.
\end{itemize}

For replication $s$, define $e_s=\widehat\theta_s-\theta_s$.  Among $S$
returned values,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Bias}=S^{-1}\sum_se_s,\qquad
\operatorname{RMSE}=\sqrt{S^{-1}\sum_se_s^2},\qquad
\operatorname{MCSE}(\operatorname{Bias})=
\frac{\operatorname{sd}(e_s)}{\sqrt S}. \label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
Metric interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bias:} direction of average error.
\item \textbf{RMSE:} main accuracy measure; combines bias and dispersion and
penalizes large errors.  Compare procedures within an estimand because scales
differ.
\item \textbf{MCSE(bias):} Monte Carlo noise in reported bias, not empirical
standard error in one data set.
\end{itemize}

Status rules:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{KSS and BS20 pass:} PyTwoWay returns a value.
\item \textbf{BLM pass:} complete static support and no one-step likelihood
decline above $10^{-4}$.
\item \textbf{Project pass:} best fit converges.  At positive rank, at least
two starts lie within $0.0001\max(1,O^*)$ of the best objective $O^*$, and
near-best spreads in $\QF$, $\HF$, and $\Cassign$ are at most
$0.001\max(1,|\theta^*|)$.
\item The project thresholds check numerical reproducibility; they are not
statistical critical values.  $\rhoH$ is derived from $\QF$ and $\HF$, so it
does not receive a separate spread test.
\item \textbf{Headline:} all-returned RMSE.  Pass-only RMSE is a diagnostic
sensitivity calculation, not a replacement estimator.
\end{itemize}

Display order and purpose:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Status:} numerical reliability.
\item \textbf{Own-model benchmarks:} implementation under correct specification.
\item \textbf{Common-target RMSE:} comparison of defensibly mapped procedures.
\item \textbf{Native-minus-project gaps:} estimation error versus target difference.
\item \textbf{Rank selection:} model-dimension performance.
\item \textbf{All-returned versus pass-only RMSE:} warning-related tail risk.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Native targets versus project targets}

For procedure $p$, the common-target error decomposes exactly as
\begin{equation}
\widehat\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}}
=(\widehat\theta_p-\theta_p)
+(\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}}). \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
The two terms are:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\widehat\theta_p-\theta_p$: native estimation error;
\item $\theta_p-\theta_{\mathrm{proj}}$: estimand gap, present before panel
sampling.
\end{itemize}
Figure~\ref{fig:gaps} plots the second term.  Subtraction isolates a difference
in definitions; it does not subtract an estimate from an arbitrary quantity.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{reports/dgp_estimator_matrix_cluster_v2/figures/native-project-target-gaps.pdf}
\caption{Population estimand differences averaged across the 100 populations
in each DGP.  The display is needed because common-target RMSE otherwise mixes
estimation error with disagreement about the target itself.}
\label{fig:gaps}
\end{figure}

\textbf{KSS.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Native $\QF$ gaps are about: AKM, $0$; Crippa, $-0.361$; BLM,
$-0.778$; low rank, $-1.093$; GKLP, $-0.693$.
\item Native $\QF$ bias is only $-0.00053$ to $0.00061$.  Thus the main
common-target error under nonadditivity is omitted interaction variation, not
poor estimation of the additive projection.
\item Under independent assignment, Equation~\eqref{eq:qdecomp} gives
$\Var_q(b_j)-\QF=-\HF/2\le0$.  With sorted assignment, no universal sign result
holds; the negative simulated signs follow from this design.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{BS20.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Native covariance exceeds $\Cassign$ by $0.414$ to $0.805$; native
covariance RMSE is only $0.00867$ to $0.02960$.
\item In the additive case, define
$Tb(i)=\E[b_J\mid I=i]$ and $T^*a(j)=\E[a_I\mid J=j]$.  Expanding
Equation~\eqref{eq:bsnative} gives
\end{itemize}
\begin{equation}
\CBS-\Cassign
=\lVert Tb\rVert_p^2+\lVert T^*a\rVert_q^2
+\langle Tb,TT^*a\rangle_p\ge0. \label{eq:bsgap}
\end{equation}
The inequality uses contraction of conditional expectation and
$x^2+y^2-xy\ge0$.  It explains the positive AKM gap.  No corresponding
universal sign result holds for a general nonadditive schedule.

tests BS20 against $\rhoBS$ and $\CBS$ without implying that either equals
$\Cassign$.

\subsection{Numerical warnings and squared-error tails}

Figure~\ref{fig:tails} compares unconditional all-returned RMSE with RMSE among
diagnostic passes.  RMSE squares errors, so a few extreme completions can
dominate it.  Pass-only is a sensitivity check, not an alternative estimator.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.90\textwidth]{reports/dgp_estimator_matrix_cluster_v2/figures/project-returned-vs-stable-rmse.pdf}
\caption{Project plug-in RMSE among all returned values and conditional on a
numerical pass.  The logarithmic scale shows that warning fits, rather than
ordinary sampling noise, generate the large unconditional errors.}
\label{fig:tails}
\end{figure}

Tail concentration for $\QF$:
\begin{itemize}
\item Median absolute error is $0.0114$ (Crippa), $0.0105$ (BLM), and $0.0159$
(continuous low rank).
\item Maximum absolute error is 480, 498, and 230, respectively.
\item The five largest replications produce 91.1\%, 100\%, and 96.9\% of total
squared error.  Typical accuracy therefore coexists with poor unconditional
risk.
\end{itemize}

Numerical evidence:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Continuous low rank:} median worst local condition number is 42
for 93 passes and 84,006 for 7 warnings; median maximum factor norm is 4.32
versus 184.9.
\item \textbf{BLM:} corresponding condition numbers are 157 and 6,340.
\item \textbf{Crippa:} pass and warning medians are 98 and 117, but the warning
90th percentile is 2.53 million; the warning 90th-percentile maximum factor
norm is 154.  Some warnings are mild; a smaller subset drives the RMSE tail.
\end{itemize}

Mechanism:
\begin{enumerate}
\item At rank $r$, each worker ALS update regresses on $[1,v_j']$ for visited
firms.
\item Visited-firm vectors may be nearly collinear.  The local Gram matrix is
then nearly singular, and its pseudoinverse amplifies small match-mean noise.
\item Large worker or firm factors propagate to unobserved pairs during
completion.  More periods can add information; centering changes only
normalization and cannot add independent local factor variation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Keep DGP and estimator separate.} KSS succeeds under AKM; BLM
succeeds under the discrete BLM DGP.  Other rows measure the cost of their
restrictions.
\item \textbf{Keep native and project targets separate.} KSS estimates an
additive projection; BS20 estimates $\CBS$, not $\Cassign$; BLM compresses a
continuous schedule into six cells.  Common-target RMSE is meaningful only
after these mappings and gaps are stated.
\item \textbf{Rank selection is not the remaining problem.} BIC selects the
true rank in all populations.  Completion can still be locally ill-conditioned
at the correct rank.
\item \textbf{The current plug-in is a baseline, not the final LOO procedure.}
The completion step needs a declared safeguard, such as regularized local
regressions, conditioning-aware support, or direct estimation of target
functionals.  Section~3.5 separates baseline calibration from untested
variants.  No current result shows that a proposed regularization improves
efficacy.
\end{enumerate}
