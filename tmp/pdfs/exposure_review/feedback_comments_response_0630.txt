2 Comment 0.2: Continuous-radius rings versus nonregular cases
Diagnosis. The comment is valid. The intended distinction should not be “ring versus smooth
kernel.” The correct distinction is “continuous parameter with differentiability” versus “genuinely
discrete selection.”
A ring exposure indexed by a continuous radius θ ∈ Θ ⊂ R can be a regular problem if the
shell mass and mean differentiability conditions hold. In that case, θ 7→ µ(θ) has a derivative, and
Wald-type inference for the continuous radius is internally consistent. By contrast, a finite grid of
radii or an integer K-hop network count is a discrete model-selection problem. There is no local
derivative with respect to the discrete index unless one embeds the discrete choices in a separate
continuous approximation.
Proposed correction. The remark should be rewritten along these lines.
The Riesz-derivative geometry applies to exposure parameters that vary over a contin-
uous set and satisfy the mean-differentiability and stochastic equicontinuity conditions.
This includes continuous ring radii when the relevant shell conditions are imposed. It
does not apply directly to genuinely discrete choices, such as a fixed finite grid of radii
or an integer K-hop exposure count. Those cases should instead be handled by the
finite-grid or selection theory.
Why this fixes the problem. The revision keeps the continuous ring specification on the regular
side when the stated differentiability assumptions are verified, while still assigning finite grids and
integer hop counts to the nonregular/discrete side.

An = {0, 1}3, Wn = {(1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1)}.

Recommended notation
Use different symbols for the two objects. For example:
An for the ambient assignment space , Wn = supp(Dn) for the design support .
Then state Section 2.1 as follows.
Let An denote the ambient assignment space. For binary treatment, An = {0, 1}Nn;
for multivalued or continuous treatment, An is the corresponding product space. The
assignment design Dn is a probability law on An, and Wn = supp( Dn) denotes the
set of assignments that can occur under the design. All design-based expectations and
conditional expectations are taken with respect to Dn. Exposure sufficiency is imposed
on Wn, equivalently Dn-almost surely.
Then define the null as
H0 : there exist θ0 ∈ Θ and eYi such that Yi(W ) = eYi{gi(W ; θ0)} Dn-a.s. for every i.
For finite designs this is equivalent to
Yi(w) = eYi{gi(w; θ0)} for every w ∈ W n.
Bottom line. The ambient assignment space is where potential outcomes can be conceptually
indexed. The design support is where the experiment generates data and where design-based
moments are evaluated. For the paper’s testing and moment results, the correct quantifier is over
supp(Dn), or equivalently Dn-almost surely, not over the full assignment cube unless the design
has full support.
