Remaining Issues in the June 15 Draft
June 15
Issues still present
Exposure sufficiency, conditional independence, and identification remain blurred
(pp. 3–4, 11, 22, 64–67)
The draft now states a strong potential-outcome sufficiency condition and adds uniqueness/minimality
language. But it still sometimes compresses three different claims:
•validity of residualized moments under a sufficient exposure map;
•conditional independence under the design law,Y i ⊥W|G i;
•point identification by the finite dictionary used empirically.
Lemma C.2 characterizes conditional independence, not automatically the pointwise restriction
Yi(w) = eYi(g(w)) for every assignment. The introduction also still leans too hard on “randomization
alone.” Randomization supplies variation; the null is exposure sufficiency, and inference also uses
identification, dependence, and regularity.
One fix that AI gave was to state the chain explicitly: Hypothesis 2.5 implies conditional inde-
pendence, which implies moment validity. Lemma C.2 gives equivalence between the full product-
moment class and conditional independence under the design law. Finite dictionaries identify
only under finite-dictionary uniqueness or minimality assumptions. Replace “randomization alone”
language with a statement that randomization is the source of variation given the maintained
exposure-sufficiency null.
Hard-ring continuous Wald inference remains only conditionally justified (pp.
17, 28–30, 49–51, 56–57, 82–85; Figure 1 on p. 29)
Problem.The draft now has a shell-regularity ULLN for rings and acknowledges that sample
ring criteria are step functions. But the Muralidharan application still reports continuous-radius
Wald intervals from a 0.1 km grid. This requires more than a fine grid: it needs a differentiable
population moment map, local stochastic equicontinuity, and the projected estimating equation.
Figure 1 also shows irregular objective shapes, so the Wald intervals may look more precise than
the theory warrants.
Could solve by choosing one interpretation. If the exercise is continuous-radius inference, verify
the shell smoothness, derivative, equicontinuity, and projected-equation conditions for the applica-
tion. If it is finite-grid/model-selection inference, report support sets, smallest-nonrejected radii,
or profile confidence sets rather than scalar Wald intervals.

Resolved or not relevant anymore
VC uniform LLN and ring VC complexity.
The old VC route for rings was removed. Rings now use shell regularity, and smooth kernels use
Lipschitz primitives.
Signed third-moment condition.
Not counted as a remaining problem. The condition is imported from the cited affinity-set CLT;
without checking that theorem, the objection is not independently valid.
Off-true moments for Hilbert-space completion elements.
Resolved. The draft now says off-θpaths and just-identified estimators are used only for span
directions, whileGis extended toHby continuity.
Nonzero-information condition for Theorem C.11.
Resolved. Assumption C.8 now explicitly requires nonzero first-order information, equivalently
∥η⋆∥Ω >0.
Finite-nversus limiting derivatives.
Resolved. Footnote 15 clarifies thatG M is the restriction of the limiting derivative functional and
states the interchange condition if finite-nderivatives are used.
Growing-sieve CLT not stated.
Resolved as a formal proof issue. Assumption C.15(S3) now explicitly assumes the changing-
direction CLT; the remaining issue is that the assumption is high-level.
Random envelope bound in the old LLN proof.
Resolved. The new proofs use bounded moment contributions and trace/covariance bounds.

Resolved for Appendix C. Lemma C.5 invokes theL 2 projection property only in the square-
integrable setting.
