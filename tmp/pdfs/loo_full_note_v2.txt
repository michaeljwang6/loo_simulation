The econometric difficulty is that none of these quantities is directly observed. Administrative
data reveal wages on the edges of a sparse bipartite worker–firm graph. The target functionals
generally put weight on counterfactual pairs. Moreover, worker effects, firm effects, and flexible
interaction factors are incidental parameters: each is learned from a small number of observations,
so plug-in quadratic functionals inherit a bias that need not vanish as the number of workers and
firms grows. Additive leave-out methods exploit a fixed linear design (Kline, Saggio, and Sølvsten,
2020). Grouped approaches obtain tractability by reducing heterogeneity to a finite number of types
(Bonhomme, Lamadon, and Manresa, 2019). Scalar shared-factor models focus on the interaction
coefficient rather than the high-dimensional wage functionals (Crippa, 2025). Paired-mover designs
identify a particular match-variance quantity for a selected mover population (Sorkin and Warwar,
2026). The missing layer is feasible inference for invariant functionals of a richer nonadditive wage
schedule while preserving free worker and firm heterogeneity.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it develops a parameterization- invariant inequality
accounting beyond additive worker–firm effects. The objects in equations (3) to (5) generalize the
firm variance, the common-effect restriction, and the worker–firm covariance, and the identities in
equations (8) and (10) reveal the economic channels suppressed by AKM. Second, it makes the

sufficient conditions for identification of any linear or quadratic functional, shows why the headline

unrestricted ignorance and cardinal low-rank completion. Within the low-rank class, connectedness
identifies additive contrasts, interaction contrasts require observed rectangles or richer overlap,
and assignment functionals require support for the counterfactual pairs weighted by the chosen
independence benchmark. Third, it develops a computable bias correction and inference procedure
for invariant linear and quadratic wage-schedule functionals under sparse mobility, proportional
growth, and bounded firm size. The minimum viable technical result is a free-factor rank-one
theorem; higher rank is an extension rather than a condition for the core paper.

The interpretation is deliberately bounded. The baseline object is a maintained systematic wage
schedule. Under additional exclusion, stability, and transportability restrictions, the same schedule
may receive a causal potential-wage interpretation. Moving from wages to productive sorting
or welfare requires a model of output, amenities, search, meetings, acceptance, bargaining, and
equilibrium feasibility. The paper measures the wage consequences of worker–firm assignment; it
does not infer the surplus consequences from wages alone.
The remainder of the paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 separates the wage schedule, assignment,
and causal or structural interpretations. Section 3 develops Q , H , and their exact decomposition.

Section 4 generalizes the AKM covariance and distinguishes several assignment channels. Section 5
establishes the unrestricted and monotone benchmarks, including target-specific impossibility results.
Section 6 then introduces the low-rank representation and states what cardinal completion it is
intended to purchase. Section 7 studies target-specific graph support within that class. Sections 8
and 9 describe estimation, bias correction, and the theorem agenda. Sections 10 and 11 lay out the
Monte Carlo and empirical implementation. The appendices contain the structural representation
map, selection taxonomy, graph details, diagnostic algebra, and full derivations that would distract

6.2 Algebraic rank versus effective dimension
The term “low rank” is ambiguous in this literature. Algebraic rank concerns the shape of the I×J
interaction matrix. Effective dimension concerns the number of free parameters used to describe it.
A free-factor rank-one matrix
Mh = uv′
has algebraic rank one but I +J −1 free factor parameters after scale normalization. It is therefore
high dimensional and subject to an incidental-parameter problem when worker and firm degrees
remain bounded.
Grouped type models restrict both concepts. If workers have K types and firms have L classes, a
block-constant interaction has at most KL cell means and rank at most min(K,L)−1 after double
centering. When K and L are fixed, there is no node-level incidental-parameter problem in the
interaction. The free-factor model deliberately declines that effective-dimension restriction. The
need for bias correction is therefore the cost of preserving continuous, node-level heterogeneity
rather than an accidental complication.

7.3 Anchor and propagation
A sufficient rank-one identification argument has three steps. First, find an anchor rectangle with

add a new worker whenever that worker is observed at two already anchored firms with distinct
firm factors; the two equations identify the worker loading and additive effect. Third, add a new
firm whenever that firm is observed for two already anchored workers with distinct worker factors.
Repeating the process propagates the schedule.

8.6 Computability
A practically relevant method cannot require one full nonlinear refit per worker, firm, or edge.
The target is near-linear or mildly superlinear complexity in the number of observed edges per
outer iteration. The implementation should use sparse normal equations, node-level Hessian blocks,
iterative linear solvers, and low-rank matrix identities. Approximate diagonal and selected off-
diagonal entries of the inverse Hessian can be obtained through Hutchinson-type trace estimators or
graph-local message passing if exact sparse factorization is infeasible.
The computation section should report wall-clock time, memory use, convergence, and sensitivity to
solver tolerance. These are part of the contribution in a setting where an asymptotically valid but
computationally prohibitive correction has little empirical value.
8.7 Rank selection and regularization
The baseline theory conditions on a fixed rank. Empirically, rank may be chosen using held-out edge
prediction, information criteria adjusted for effective degrees of freedom, or stability of the headline
functionals. Regularization can stabilize weakly connected factors, but it changes the estimand
unless its bias is removed or made asymptotically negligible. The preferred presentation reports
fixed-rank results for r = 0,1,2,... and treats the selected-rank estimate as a summary rather than
hiding the sensitivity.

12 Interpretation: Wage Differences, Selection, and Sorting
12.1 What the estimates establish
The estimates establish the magnitude and heterogeneity of employer-related systematic wage
differences under a maintained schedule. They can reveal that a common firm premium is a good or
poor summary of those differences, and they can measure how the observed assignment changes
the wage distribution relative to a declared benchmark. They do not identify why the schedule has
that shape. Comparative advantage, bargaining, monopsony, rent sharing, amenities, and selective
acceptance can all generate similar wage patterns.
12.2 Four meanings of sorting
The word “sorting” should be reserved for a stated object. Four distinct quantities recur in the
literature:

H Two Cycle Spaces and Graph Diagnostics
Kline-style mover cycles live on a projected firm graph whose edges are average mover contrasts.
Additivefirmeffectsimplythatthesumofdirectededgeeffectsaroundeverycycleiszero. Interaction
rectangles live on the original bipartite worker–firm graph and compare two workers at two firms.
The two graph structures are related but not identical.
A sample can have many movers connecting firms and therefore rich projected cycles, yet few pairs
of workers observed at the same pair of firms. It then supports additive firm-effect diagnostics but
weakly identifies free interactions. Conversely, repeated worker pairs at a dense subset of firms can
identify local interactions while leaving the global firm graph fragile. Empirical diagnostics should
therefore report both cycle spaces.
The shared-factor Tukey model also has a special polynomial cycle structure because the interaction
parameter enters the factorization itself. That cycle-length difficulty should not be mechanically
transferred to the free-factor rank-one model, where rectangle equations are bilinear in free factor
differences and propagation after gauge fixing can be linear. The necessity theorem remains a
matrix-completion problem on the observed rectangle mask.
